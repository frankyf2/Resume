CareVentis documents and offers traditional Malay healing: urut tradisional, terapi tarik bisa and urutan pembetulan tulang. The site explains each tradition and the circumstances people come in with, keeps a knowledge library, and lets people book an appointment online. My manager came up with the idea; I designed and built all of it, the public site and the admin behind it.

The public website

Written for people who want to understand a practice before they book it.

Services and traditions

What is offered, and where it comes from.

  • Services index with a page for each of the three therapies
  • Heritage Traditions and Health Circumstances, each with an index and detail pages
  • Home, About, FAQ, Contact, and Privacy, Terms and Disclaimer pages

Knowledge

A library, not just a blog.

  • Articles with categories, a Research Library, Resources and a Press page
  • A public “share your story” form, held for review and rate-limited

Booking

From picking a service to the reminder.

  • A month calendar per service showing which days are open, full or closed, built from one database query
  • Hourly slots with already-booked and past times removed, within a booking window set by staff
  • Confirmation email with an Add to Google Calendar link and a signed .ics file with a two-hour reminder
  • A Manage My Booking link to view, reschedule or cancel, and a reminder email the day before

Member area

One place for a returning client.

  • Register with an emailed verification code, sign in and reset a password
  • Dashboard with my appointments, inquiries, comments and stories

The admin system

Built so the clinic can run bookings, content and the site itself without asking a developer.

Dashboard

Today at a glance.

  • Next appointments, latest inquiries and charts
  • Loaded once in the background after each deploy, so the first admin visit isn't slow

Appointments

The clinic's diary.

  • Calendar by day or week, or a list
  • Mark as completed, or cancel a booking

Inquiries

Every message from the site.

  • Internal notes only admins can see
  • Reply templates for common answers

Articles and comments

Publishing with review.

  • Editor with slug, category, byline, excerpt, publish date and draft or live
  • SEO meta title and description that fall back to sensible defaults
  • Reject a public submission with a note that's emailed to the submitter; comment moderation

Members

Who has an account.

  • Members, admins and super admins; locked and deactivated accounts; last sign-in

Emails, visits and activity

What the site did, and who used it.

  • Email outbox: waiting, sent and failed, with each email readable
  • Visitor statistics: top pages, devices, where visitors came from and engagement, for today, 7, 30 or 90 days or 12 months
  • Activity: sign-ins and admin actions

Settings and data

Changed live, no redeploy.

  • Business hours, closed weekdays and holidays, and how far ahead people can book; changes apply to the next lookup
  • Data and retention: how long each log is kept, table sizes, and a history of cleanups

Under the hood

The parts a visitor never sees but the clinic depends on.

Email that gets there

An outbox, not a fire-and-forget send.

  • Every email is queued first; a worker retries failures at 1, 2, 5, 15 and 30 minutes, then up to 24 hours
  • A row being sent is leased, so a restart mid-send never sends it twice, and stale emails expire

Housekeeping

Running unattended.

  • A nightly retention job at 03:30 Malaysia time, which catches up after a restart
  • Error alerts emailed when errors pile up or a background job fails, with a cooldown so nobody gets flooded
  • Health checks and a cleaner for abandoned uploads

Security

Defaults that hold up.

  • Content-Security-Policy and security headers, switchable between enforce, report-only and off without a restart, with a report endpoint
  • Cloudflare Turnstile and a sliding-window rate limiter on public forms
  • BCrypt passwords and sign-in lockouts

Behind Cloudflare

Knowing who the visitor really is.

  • A Cloudflare Worker forwards the real visitor IP and country with a shared secret, and the app only trusts those headers when the secret matches
